Why we are quicker

A design in minutes. A kit in days, not seasons.

A custom brick model usually takes months, because somebody is drawing it by hand and you are in a queue behind everybody else. We took the drawing out of the queue.

The design is done the day you ask

The studio draws the first version while you watch, and a person works through it with you the same day. There is no waiting list, because there is no backlog of drawing to get through.

The instructions write themselves

Every step and every numbered bag comes out of the model itself — nineteen thousand bricks sorted into build order in about a second. Nobody draws a booklet by hand here.

Every piece is a piece LEGO sells

The design is checked against LEGO's own shop while it is being made, so ordering the bricks is one upload rather than a hunt through substitutes. Nothing gets designed that cannot be bought.

Which leaves the post as the only real wait. Once you are happy with the design, the bricks are ordered from LEGO's Pick a Brick shop and their delivery is the long pole — usually a week or two. Large models take longer, because LEGO caps how many of one piece you may buy at a time and the order has to go in more than once.

Questions

Before you ask

How long does it take?

The design comes back in minutes, and a person works through it with you the same day. After that the wait is LEGO's rather than ours: the bricks come from their Pick a Brick shop and usually take a week or two, while the instructions are generated the moment the design is settled. A very large model takes longer, because LEGO caps how many of one piece you may buy at once and the order goes in more than once. A hand-drawn commission elsewhere takes months — the drawing is the part we removed.

What do you need from me?

Five pictures: the front straight on, both corners, the back, and one from above — the aerial view is the only way to get a roof right. If you have none to hand, the address is enough and we will look up the map views ourselves.

Do you keep my photographs?

Only while your quote is open, so the person designing it can see them. When a quote is closed or declined, the pictures are deleted. Designs made in the studio drop their photographs as soon as the design exists.

Why Voxeloom?

A voxel is a pixel with depth — the grid of coloured cells every model begins as, before a single brick is chosen. A loom is what turns loose thread into something a family keeps. Your house makes that journey here: a grid on a screen on Monday, a box of numbered bags by the end of the month.

Are these real LEGO bricks?

Yes. Every part is ordered from LEGO's own Pick a Brick shop, so your model is built from genuine LEGO® bricks and can be repaired with them years from now. We are an independent workshop, not part of the LEGO Group.
